I am an applied mathematician who is passionate about modelling complex systems using probabilistic and statistical tools.

During my PhD at MaIAGE, INRAE under the supervision of Patrick Hoscheit, Elisabeta Vergu and Estelle Kuhn, I focused on developing spatially embedded probabilistic models to help understand the commercial cattle network in France using random graph models. I was particularly interested in the spread of epidemics on random graphs, as well as parameter inference using approximate statistical methods based on simulations.

My research in plain words

How do animal diseases spread through the networks that move livestock between farms? I build mathematical models of these trade networks — maps where farms are connected by the animals they exchange — and use them to simulate epidemics and understand what makes outbreaks grow or fade. The goal is to give veterinarians and policymakers better tools to anticipate and control infectious diseases in farm animals.

Recent Publications

  1. The contact process on Scale-Free Percolation

    Andrée Barnier, Patrick Hoscheit, Michele Salvi, Elisabeta Vergu

    Preprint · 14 May 2025 · arXiv:2505.10582

    We consider the contact process on scale-free percolation, a spatial random graph model where the degree distribution of the vertices follows a power law with exponent β. We study the extinction time τGn of the contact process on the graph restricted to a d-dimensional box of volume n, starting from full occupancy. In the regime β ∈ (2,3), where the degrees have finite mean but infinite variance and the graph exhibits the ultra-small world behaviour, we adapt existing techniques to show that τGn is exponential in n. Our main contribution, though, deals with the case β ≥ 3, where the degrees have finite variance and the graph is small-world. We prove that also in this case τGn grows exponentially, at least up to a logarithmic correction reflecting the sparser graph structure. The proof requires the generalization of a previous result and combines a multi-scale analysis of the graph, the study of the chemical distance between vertices and percolation arguments.

    L'île d'Oléron · Presentation of the preliminary work done in my first year of PhD

    About the conference

    The Young Probabilists and Statisticians Conference has been held every two years since 1994 under the auspices of the Random Modelling and Statistics (MAS) group of the Society for Applied and Industrial Mathematics (SMAI). Participants are advanced PhD students (in their second or third year) or those who may have defended their thesis within the past year. The aim is to provide them with a pleasant and informal setting in which to meet, present their work and broaden their range of interests.
